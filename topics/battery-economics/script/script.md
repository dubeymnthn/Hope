# Documentary Script: Why Modern EV Batteries Are Still Expensive: The Cathode Choke

*Total Words:* 145 | *Estimated Duration:* ~1 mins

## Cold Open Hook
> For a decade, the auto industry promised that hundred-dollar battery packs would make electric cars cheaper than gasoline. But that curve just hit an impenetrable wall.

## Narrative Scenes
### Scene 1: `scene-001` — Establish the stubborn battery pack price plateau at 139 dollars per kilowatt hour
> "Between 2010 and 2020, battery costs plummeted by nearly ninety percent. But today, average electric vehicle battery packs have stalled out near one hundred and thirty-nine dollars per kilowatt hour."

*Visual Hint:* Statistic card showing $139/kWh average pack cost plateau curve

### Scene 2: `scene-002` — Contrast manufacturing labor overhead against raw mineral domination at 72 percent
> "The reason isn't factory inefficiency. Gigafactories are more automated than ever. The trap is that raw cathode minerals have surged from forty percent to over seventy-two percent of total cell costs."

*Visual Hint:* Comparison breakdown showing bill of materials shift from 40% to 72% raw precursors

### Scene 3: `scene-003` — Trace the historic cost curve from 1100 dollars down to the current inflection
> "From the eleven-hundred dollar compliance cars of 2010 to the mass market inflection of 2020, price cuts came easy. But as spot mineral markets erupted, the low-hanging fruit completely evaporated."

*Visual Hint:* Milestone timeline tracking battery cost deflation and the commodity inflection point

### Scene 4: `scene-004` — Expose the geographic refining choke point controlling 75 percent of precursor chemicals
> "Compounding the chemical cost, China controls seventy-five percent of global lithium chemical refining and eighty-five percent of synthetic graphite. Building localized supply chains carries a massive capital penalty."

*Visual Hint:* Technical schematic detailing the precursor refining bottleneck and international tariff walls

### Scene 5: `scene-005` — Synthesize the thermodynamic floor and conclude with the future outlook
> "Until solid-state chemistry achieves mass automotive yields in the late 2020s, the battery remains less like consumer electronics, and more like an expensive refined industrial asset."

*Visual Hint:* Kinetic typography framing the chemical cost reality of the clean energy transition


## Closing Takeaway
> The electric vehicle revolution will not be won by software. It will be decided in the chemistry of the cathode.
